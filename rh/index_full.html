Certify

ATD metrics

ATD hit rate — share of his 2026 games with at least one rushing or receiving TD (passing TDs and return TDs don't count, matching how the contract settles). TD/g — rushing + receiving TDs per game.

Red-zone opportunity share — his carries + targets inside the opponent's 20, divided by his team's red-zone carries + targets in the games he played. RZ opps/g — the same opportunities per game.

Target share — his targets ÷ team targets in his games. Red-zone target share — the same, inside the 20. Sacks, spikes, kneels, two-point tries and nullified plays are excluded.

ATD score — the average of his percentile ranks on ATD hit rate, TD/g, red-zone opportunity share, target share and red-zone target share, each measured against a league-wide pool of players with 3+ games and at least 2 carries + targets per game (equal weights). Hit rate − price — his ATD hit rate minus the Yes price, in points; a rough gap, not a probability estimate.

How the NBA screen and outlook work

Lines. Every Robinhood NBA contract (Kalshi's order book, which Robinhood routes to) for games from Sat Oct 24 to Tue Oct 27: moneylines, spreads, totals, team totals, and player points, rebounds, assists, threes and points + rebounds + assists. Robinhood lists player props about a day ahead, so props cover tonight's games so far.

Hit rate. Player props: his last 20 games (2025-26 and 2026-27, any team). Team lines: that team's last 20 games; game totals: both teams' last 20. A line qualifies when its Yes price is 45–55¢ and the condition came true in more than half of those games (at least 10).

Outlook. Minutes cases: his games split into thirds by minutes, weighted toward this season and his last 15 games; each case's chance is his hit rate in those games nudged toward his overall rate, and tonight's case odds lean on his recent minutes and shift toward the bear case when the spread makes a blowout likelier than usual. Projection: the case average times tonight's adjustments (defense vs his position, pace, game script from the spread, implied team total, back-to-back, teammates out with his with/without record), capped at −25% / +30%. Our read averages the cases' chance with a gamma (points, PRA) or negative-binomial (rebounds, assists, threes) distribution priced at the projection, shrunk toward 50%; Wedge % = 70% the books' price for that rung + 30% our read. Injuries: CBS Sports injury report, Fri Oct 23 morning. Live record: each posted line settled against the final ESPN box score. Data: ESPN box scores via hoopR, Action Network book lines, Kalshi.

How the backtest works

Which lines. Every Robinhood contract from Weeks 1–5 whose last trade before kickoff was 45–55¢. Price history comes from Robinhood's hourly price data; a contract that never traded before kickoff has no real price and is left out.

What's covered. Moneyline, winning margin, overtime, 1st half / full time and most receiving yards for all five weeks, plus spreads and totals for Weeks 3–5. Robinhood doesn't expose the Weeks 1–2 spread and total markets, Week 2 moneylines, or any past player props and quarter/half lines, so those aren't in the sample.

Factors. Favorite / underdog uses the pre-kickoff moneyline price (Week 2, which has no moneyline, uses the winning-margin prices); totals and overtime have no side. Liquidity is contracts traded in that line's final three days. Results are settled against ESPN final scores and box scores; return assumes one Yes contract per line at the listed price, before fees.

How it works

The lines
Every Robinhood NFL line, updated Sat Nov 7 at 2:25 PM CT. Where Robinhood has no line yet, we price a matching one from five sportsbooks (Source: Books · RH-style).
Hit rate
How often the line came true in 2026: in his games for player props, in the team's games for team lines, in either team's games for totals.
Wedge %
Our chance the line comes true: the price, adjusted for how often that kind of line beat its price in Weeks 6–8. Passing overs have come true more often than priced; rushing and receiving-yard overs less often.
Picks
The side where Wedge % beats the price by 3 points or more, on Robinhood's own lines priced 45–55¢. Posted picks went 248–190 (56.6%) over Weeks 6–8, +4.0% before fees.
Reads by game
Each game's reads rank Robinhood's own player props, the better side priced 25–75¢, by the best balance of chance and price (Kelly growth): how far Wedge % clears the price, weighed by how likely it is to hit. Wedge % was fit on lines priced 40–60¢, so reads outside that range are marked as a rough guide.
The read
Select a player prop for his games since 2023, the matchup, the game script, the weather and our projection.
Flags
“No bid”: only an ask is posted. “Wide spread”: more than 5¢ between bid and ask. “Small sample”: fewer than 3 games.
Full method notes and model history

Team lines (moneyline, spreads, winning margin, quarter/half winners and spreads, 1st-half/full-time) are checked against that team's own 2026 games. Game totals, quarter/half totals, ties and overtime are checked against every 2026 game either team has played. Player props are checked against that player's 2026 games, including games he played with no stat line (counted as zero).

Prop categories: positions and teams come from current ESPN rosters. The favorite is the side with the higher Robinhood moneyline (Sat Nov 7 ~2:25 PM CT). Player props Robinhood doesn't list use the Robinhood-style rungs priced from the sportsbooks described on the Week 9 lines tab. Defense softness counts rushing and receiving production by opposing RBs/FBs and by opposing WRs/TEs (PPR: 1 per catch, 0.1 per yard, 6 per TD). Each prop sits in its most specific group, so a prop in tab 3 is not also in tab 1.

Model update (Sun Oct 25). On 385 settled Week 6–7 lines priced 40–60¢, the Yes side came true 45% of the time against a 50¢ average price; buying every Yes lost 13%, buying every No made 9%. The >50% hit-rate filter didn't help (44.5% vs 45.8%), and the old Wedge % ran about 5 points high. Wedge % is now recalibrated on those lines: a logit shift by market (rushing and anytime-TD overs down about 9 points at 50¢, receiving about 2, passing up about 8) and for players with under 12 games for their current team (down about 12). 5-fold cross-validated Brier 0.2417 vs 0.2490 before (price alone 0.2505); trained on Week 6 and tested on Week 7, 0.2345 vs 0.2394. The chip now names the better side (Yes or No) when it clears its price by 3+ points. Two weeks is a small sample; the shift is re-fit as each week settles.

Live check and re-fit (Mon Oct 26). Sunday's 12 games were the first real test: 487 lines posted at ~11:56 AM, before kickoff. Wedge picks (best side, 3+ pts) went 148–101 (59.4%), +5.4% on what they cost: No picks 144–92 (+6.5%), Yes picks 4–9 (−22%); picks with 8+ pts of edge 68–47 (+11.5%). For comparison, every Yes −5.7%, every No +1.3%, the >50% hit-rate filter's Yes picks 42–54 (−13.4%). As a probability, though, v2 ran too low: on 40–60¢ lines it averaged 43% and they came true 47.8% (price 49.8¢), Brier 0.2558 vs 0.2529 before and 0.2523 for the price; rushing overs were the worst miss (36% vs 44%), and the thin-history penalty flipped (those players hit 29% in Week 6, 49% in Week 7). Re-fit on all 678 settled Week 6–7 lines with the thin term dropped: at 50¢, receiving 46%, rushing 41%, anytime TD 43%, passing 58%. Trained on one week and tested on the other it beats the old blend and the price both ways (Week 7: 0.2497 vs 0.2506 and 0.2511; Week 6: 0.2461 vs 0.2508 and 0.2513). The margins are small and two weeks is still a small sample. DAL @ PHI uses the re-fit; finished games keep their posted numbers.

Model v3 and the momentum study (Tue Oct 27). Model v5 and the Weeks 6–8 backtest (Tue Nov 3). We settled every Week 8 line we posted (894 lines across all 15 games, from nflverse box scores and snap counts) and re-tested the model on all 1,333 settled Week 6–8 player lines at real prices, holding out one week at a time. Seven problems turned up. (1) Our matchup read pointed the wrong way: how far it leaned from the price was negatively correlated with how lines settled in every week (−0.03, −0.04, −0.08; −0.29 on rushing). (2) We calibrated the blend of market and read when the price itself was the better thing to calibrate: shifting the Robinhood price by its market's over bias scored a Brier of 0.2460 held out, vs 0.2471 for last week's model and 0.2493 for the raw price (Week 8 alone: 0.2459 vs 0.2510). (3) Receptions were lumped in with receiving yards, and passing TDs and interceptions with passing yards; at about 50¢ receptions came true 48% of the time, receiving yards 45%, rushing 39–42%, passing yards 62%, interceptions 56% and passing TDs 50%. (4) Our projections missed by more than the line itself (average miss on real lines: rushing 25.4 yards vs 21.7 for the line, receiving 24.4 vs 23.2, passing 58.2 vs 56.3). (5) Thursday prices for Sunday games scored worse than Sunday-morning prices (0.2164 vs 0.2098), and late moves kept going rather than reversing; the Sunday re-price was built but never went live. (6) Picks per pull ballooned (59, 167, 275) as Kalshi's ladders grew, and bigger edges didn't win more often than small ones. (7) The certification key still said Week 7, so four Week 8 certified picks never settled; fixed, and they now show under Week 8 on the Results tab.

What changed. Wedge % = the price shown (Robinhood's own, or Robinhood-style from the books) shifted in log-odds by its market's over bias (passing yards +0.22, interceptions +0.13, passing TDs +0.01, receptions −0.08, receiving yards −0.19, rushing −0.31, anytime TD −0.02), fit on Weeks 6–8 with shrinkage; our read is still shown in the outlook. Held out a week at a time, its picks (3+ pts vs the price) went 283–208 (57.6%), +12.9% before fees; the Wedge picks actually posted in Weeks 6–8 went 248–190 (56.6%), +4.0%. Projections: we took the random-features ridge regression from Kelly, Malamud and Zhou's “The Virtue of Complexity in Return Prediction” (AQR; Journal of Finance 2024) and AQR's “Understanding the Virtue of Complexity” (2025): 3,000 random sine/cosine transforms of the inputs, fit with heavy ridge shrinkage and averaged over five draws, which picks up non-linear effects without hand-picking them. It is trained on 2023–26 player games to predict how far he lands from a book-style line, using recent volume and efficiency, his usage profile (target and carry share, depth of target), role, the spread and team total, the opposing defense's coverage, blitz and run-defense numbers, his offense's pass rate, and weather. Walked forward, it beat our old projection on passing, receiving and rushing yards and added 2–6% of explained variance over the book-style line. On real lines it improved only passing (average miss 54.7 vs 56.3 for the line), so the best estimate is the line moved toward it by a weight fit on Weeks 6–8: passing all the way, receptions half, receiving yards a quarter, rushing not at all. We also checked Moskowitz's “Asset Pricing and Sports Betting” (AQR), which finds sportsbook line moves overreact and partly reverse; on Kalshi's Week 8 props the moves continued, so v5 doesn't fade them. Three weeks is a small sample: the shifts are estimates and will move as more weeks settle.

Wedge % and the outlook: every regular-season game a player has played for his current team since 2023 (2026 through Week 8). Usage cases: his clean games split into thirds by volume (targets for receiving lines, carries for rushing, touches for TDs, dropbacks for passing); each case's chance is his hit rate in those games nudged toward his overall rate, and this week's case odds come from the splits that look like this week (QB, lineup, No. 1 competitor, defense), weighted toward 2026. Projection: the case average times this week's adjustments: defense vs his position (2026 allowed per game vs league, shrunk toward average for small samples), coverage (yards per target allowed by the defenders likely to cover him, using the injury report), game script (his results when his team won by 8+, stayed within 7 or lost by 8+, weighted by the odds from the spread), team total (implied points vs points per game) and weather (wind, cold and rain for outdoor games); combined adjustments are capped at −30% / +35%. Our read averages the cases' chance with a distribution priced at the projection, shrunk toward 50% (calibrated on 2025); Since Week 9 (model v5), Wedge % is the market price shifted by its market's over bias on Weeks 6–8 and our read is shown for reference (Week 8: 85% market and 15% our read; before that 70/30). Recent role: when his snap share over the last 4 games is 10+ points off his season share, the projection scales with the square root of his change in volume (targets, attempts) or moves 75% of the way to his last-4 rushing average, capped at −20% / +25%. Backtest: on 3,436 player lines from 2025, walked forward week by week, our read scored a Brier of 0.238 vs 0.250 for a coin flip and 0.246 for his last-8 hit rate. On 334 real Week 6 prices, Wedge % scored 0.2483 vs 0.2486 for the price alone and 0.304 for the season hit-rate screen: the market already prices most of this, so small edges are noise. Games he left early hurt and final-week games he was rested are left out. ★ marks games whose game total implies more than 49.5 points.
